# Melo Lab

A browser sketchpad for UK garage, house, deep house, melodic house and rap/grime ideas. It generates a 5-part loop (melody, counter-melody, brass, chords, bass) and shows it on an interactive 3D piano roll. You can play it, edit it, arrange it into a full song and export it as MIDI.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test           # music engine, MIDI writer, history, layout
npm run eval       # structural metrics over 400 loops, plus example MIDI files (see eval/)
npm run build      # typecheck + production build
```

## Using it

The side panel has three tabs. **Tracks** lists the parts, **Composition** holds style, phrasing, harmony and shape, and **Sound** holds the instruments, guide beat and automation. Hide the panel with `\` to give the roll the whole stage. The app is light by default; the moon button in the header switches to dark. The theme is remembered, and so is the open tab for the rest of the session.

- **Generate** (`G`) rolls a new seed. The dice on a track regenerate only that part. The lock next to the dice keeps a part through Generate and through changes of density, style, length and key (it moves with the key and repeats to fill a longer loop); while anything is locked the chords stay too. Parts you've edited by hand are marked *edited*: they stay when another part is rewritten (a new melody no longer rewrites an edited counter-melody or brass) and move, rather than being rewritten, when only the key or scale changes.
- **Takes**: under the parts, the Tracks tab offers four other takes on the active part, best first, each with a sketch and how it differs ("Busier, higher"). Click one to load it (undo goes back) or drag it into your DAW. The order comes from the scores described under *Planned, not random* (for the counter-melody: playing in the melody's gaps and under it; for the brass: under the melody and not piling in with it and the chords; for the chords: the style's offbeat stabs, smooth voice leading and nothing over the melody); trust your ears over it.
- **Style** sets the groove: tempo, swing, chord extensions, bass patterns, rhythm feel and guide beat. **Rap** (rap & grime) is 140 BPM and straight, with half-time drums: a snare on beat three, eighth-note hats broken up by triplet and 32nd rolls, and 808 lines whose notes run into each other.
- **Bars**: click a bar in the phrasing strip to lock it, which keeps every part's notes in that bar through Generate (and its chords through New chords), or to rewrite it: **Rewrite** gives another take on that one bar of the active part, using the variation moves (new notes on the same rhythm, a new rhythm, inversion, passing notes, approach notes, re-voicings), and pressing again gives another. The rest of the part stays as it was.
- **Phrasing** sets how the melody is built: *call & response* (a one-bar call, then an answer made from it by inverting, fragmenting, displacing or re-ending it), *trade* (the counter-melody plays the answers), *hook*, *question & answer*, *sequence* (the motif follows the chords) or *arc*. *Auto* picks one that suits the style. The bar strip shows each bar's role and which part carries it. Some downbeats land an eighth early, across the barline, most often in UKG.
- **Sounds**: each style has its own instruments. UKG gets a vowel-chop lead, a glass mallet, garage stabs and a driven sub. House gets a saw lead, marimba, an M1-style organ and a pluck bass. Deep house gets a velvet FM lead, a soft bell, an electric piano with a pad underneath and a warm sub. Melodic house gets a supersaw pluck, an arp, a wide pad and a rolling bass. Rap gets a square lead, bell arps, dark strings and an 808: a saturated sine that punches down an octave on fresh notes and slides when one note runs into the next. An eski square bass is there for grime. Every patch is two or three synth layers (body, transient, sub) with its own chorus, drive or tremolo. Choose a different sound for any part in the Sound tab; you'll hear a preview, and your choice stays when you change style. The guide beat has a kick built from three layers, flammed claps in a small room (over an 808-style snare for rap) and metallic hats, tuned for each style. In the house styles the music ducks on every kick like a sidechain, then swells back. Reverb and delay are tempo-synced and filtered so they don't muddy the low end.
- **Automation** suggests a few production moves that fit the idea, such as filter builds, delay throws on phrase endings, darker answers, a pumping bass filter, attack blooms, reverb washes and arp sweeps. Each lane says why it works. Switch lanes on or off, click one to see it on the deck in front of the roll (or click the deck to step through them), and use the dice for other ideas. Lanes that are on play back and export.
- **Harmony**: choose the tonic on the mini keyboard (dots mark notes in the scale), then a scale. The chord chips light up as they play. **New chords** writes a new progression and rewrites the chords, bass, counter-melody and brass over it, keeping the drums. The melody is placed over the new chords from its own plan; if it's locked, edited or uploaded it stays exactly as it is, and the progression that suits it best out of six is chosen. It's off while the chords or bass are locked or uploaded. Over an upload, the new chords also survive a change of loop length. MIDI export has no pitch bends: how far a bend goes depends on each synth's bend range, so slides are written as notes that run into each other instead.
- **Transition at the end of bar 8**: an 8-bar loop ends with one move that the drums and every part play together, so it hands over to the top of the loop or the next section. *Roll*: a clap roll over the last two beats speeds from eighths to sixteenths, the chords stab along with it, and the bass walks up into bar 1. *Stop*: the band hits once together and cuts out for the last beat or two, leaving the lead on its own (it runs into bar 1 if it would otherwise rest). *Lift*: the kick drops out for the bar, the bass and chords hold long notes, and the hats double up into the 1. *Auto* picks one for the style (stops for UKG and rap, lifts for house), and *None* turns it off. The part carrying the last phrase always keeps its line. In song mode the fill comes at the end of every 8 bars where the full beat plays.
- **Brass**: a horn section with a different job in each loop. It plays *stabs* (one rhythm chosen to lock with the groove and land where the melody rests), *answers* (a short figure in the melody's gaps, from each chord's third or seventh), *swells* (each chord held by the section, on the downbeat or a beat late) or *doubles* (the melody underneath itself, an octave down). Answers and doubles can be in one line or in thirds or sixths. It comes in on every bar, from halfway through the loop, or only in the last bar of each phrase. The Tracks tab says which. The part's seed picks all of this and the sound: *Horn stabs*, *Synth brass* or *FM trumpet*, weighted by style. In a trade the counter-melody does the answering, so the brass doesn't. It always sits under the melody, and a new melody brings a new brass part. In a stop at the end of an 8-bar loop the horns hit with the band. In a song it plays in the drops, hooks and bridge. The brass sounds can also play the melody, counter-melody or chords.
- **Different every time**: each new seed gets its own take on the style. The bass and chord rhythms start from the style's patterns and rework them: a hit nudged a sixteenth off the beat, one dropped or added in a gap, notes clipped short, held on or split. The downbeat (and beat three, where a 2-bar loop changes chord) always stays, and a new hit keeps an eighth clear of the others. The counter-melody can be an answering figure, a slow line or an arp in any style, with each style leaning to its own. Arps play eighths, a 3-3-2 push, dotted eighths or a gallop, at one of a few heights. The guide beat swaps the style's kick, hats and open hats (and rap's hat rolls) for other figures from the same style, and keeps the backbeat. Each style has more progressions to start from.
- **Planned, not random**: before any notes, a shared plan (`plan.ts`) fixes the loop's drums and, for each bar, its role in the phrase (statement, repeat, contrast, cadence), its energy, how much room the melody leaves and where the turnarounds are. Every part reads it. The bass is a two-bar figure, the second bar answering the first, picked for how it sits against the kick: in UKG it lands with some kicks, answers others a sixteenth or two later, keeps off the clap and plays short, with at most one ghost note a bar; house answers the kick more, deep house holds on. It meets some of the chord stabs, not all of them. Chord stabs are picked for the style's offbeat feel and voiced under the melody, keeping common tones. The melody follows only the plan and the harmony, so rerolling the chords' or bass's rhythm leaves it alone. A motif keeps its intervals over each chord by moving as a whole to fit, rather than snapping every note; a strong note off the chord stays only if the next note resolves it by step. Bars 2 and 3 build on bar 1, and bars the plan marks sparse play only their idea's head and leave the back half to the counter-melody. Fills, walk-ups and pattern changes only come in the last bar of each four-bar phrase. In 8-bar loops deep and melodic house sometimes let each chord ring for two bars, and deep house sometimes lays a chord over its third in the bass. Grace notes only lead into a long note on the beat, sometimes closing in from both sides (an enclosure), and octave pops fall on the weak sixteenths. Six melody takes and four bass takes are written for each loop and the one that scores best is kept, comparing only takes about as busy as the seed's own so density stays yours. The scores (`score.ts`) aim for windows, not maxima: an idea that comes back, mostly stepwise movement that recovers from leaps, strong beats on the harmony with resolved tension allowed, an ending that lands, a peak in bar 3, the style's syncopation and range; for the bass, the kick windows above, the clap, and a figure that repeats. They are structural proxies, not a judgement of taste. The counter-melody is one figure, placed in the melody's gaps and played every bar from each chord's third or seventh, so it moves only when the harmony does. Hooks are often riffs, most of all in house: a groove cell with repeated notes in two groups, where the pitch changes once, on an offbeat. The second bar keeps the rhythm and answers on one note or moves the shape, and the two bars come round exactly. A repeated note holds its pitch across the beats, and accents are set once for each sixteenth of the bar, so every repeat is played with the same feel. A varied repeat keeps the first half of the bar and changes the end. Pushed downbeats and decorations repeat wherever their bar repeats, and in a trade every call gets the same answer.
- **Feel**: generated parts are played with each style's touch (`expression.ts`): velocity follows the plan's energy from bar to bar, UKG and house chord stabs are clipped short and pushed a few ticks early, bass ghost notes sit late, deep house lines lie back, and rap stays tight. Outside rap the melody is phrased like a sung line: a held note carries across a sixteenth's gap into the next note on the beat (unless the counter-melody or brass plays there), and a long note lets go a sixteenth early before a rest. The nudges (at most 24 ticks) are applied after swing in playback and in every MIDI export; the grid and editing ignore them. Uploaded parts keep their own feel.
- **Shape**: length (2/4/8 bars), density, variation and swing. Density and variation keep the seed, so notes glide into their new places rather than starting over.
- **Creativity** (default 50%) sets how far the idea strays from the style's stock moves. At 0 you get diatonic chords, scale notes and stock rhythms. As it rises, chords pick up colours (sus, add9, 6/9, 11ths), and progressions start borrowing from the parallel mode (bVI, bVII, iv in major; a major IV or V in minor; a Phrygian bII for rap). Secondary dominants and passing chords lead into the next chord, some chords split into half bars, and the second half of an 8-bar loop may take a twist. Melodies use bolder rhythm cells and contours (arches, leaps then falls, runs in thirds), syncopation and grace notes, chromatic approaches, octave pops and a pickup into the loop. Chords push ahead of the beat and add ghost stabs. Bass lines add octave pops, approach notes, walk-ups and, for rap, more 808 slides. Melodic parts follow each borrowed chord's own scale, and chords outside the key are labelled with their numeral (bVI, V/ii). Most of the extras draw from their own random streams, so moving the slider changes the details more than the whole idea.
- **Editing**: click a note to select it and hear it. Drag a note to move it, or drag its right end to resize it. Double-click the grid to add a note to the active track; pick that track by clicking its row or pressing `1`–`5`. Arrow keys nudge the selected note. Every edit can be undone.
- **Camera**: **Isometric** is the default composed view and **Top** is a flat DAW-style view (`V` switches between them). Both stay fixed so clicks and drags always edit, and the scroll wheel zooms toward the cursor a little. **Explore** (`E`) lets you orbit, pan (right-drag) and zoom freely. `F` reframes the roll in any view. The framing is computed from the roll's size and the free stage area, so it refits when the window resizes or the panel is hidden.
- **Export MIDI** writes a format-1 `.mid` file named after the title, style, key, tempo and seed (`untitled-idea_ukg_F-minor_132bpm_seed-9BADCC51.mid`), so you can find the idea that made it. It has a tempo/key/time-signature track plus one named track per part on its own channel, padded to the full loop length. Swing is written into the note timings, so the file plays back the way it sounds in the app. Automation lanes that are on are written as CC on their part's channel: cutoff CC74, resonance CC71, attack CC73, release CC72, reverb send CC91, delay send CC94. Map those in your DAW or synth.
- **Song** (`S`, or the list button in the transport) turns the loop into a full track. Each style suggests a structure, for example intro, build, drop, breakdown, build, drop, outro for house, or intro, hook, verse, hook, verse, hook, outro for rap. Its verses leave the melody out to make room for vocals. The timeline under the roll shows each section with a row per part. Click a coloured cell to bring a part in or out, and click a drum cell to cycle the beat: off, light (no clap), full, or build (hats and a clap roll that speeds up into the next section). Click a section's header to set its type, length (a multiple of the loop), drums and filter move. A *dark* section holds the filters down; a *rise* opens them through the section. You can also move, duplicate or delete sections there. **Add section** inserts one after the selected section, and the dice suggests another structure. Every section starts the loop from its top, and each plays its own version of it, its **material**, shown on the timeline: *A* the loop as written, *A′* with new phrase endings and a bass that leans into the changes, *A″* a busier, brighter return (octave leaps, passing notes, new stabs, a busier bass), *B* a contrast made from the same notes (the melody sequenced or turned over, held chords, roots in the bass) and *R*, reduced, the bare bones. On *Auto* intros and outros are reduced, verses and builds A′, breakdowns and bridges B, the first drop or hook A and later ones A″; pick any material in the section's inspector. The last pass before a drop or hook turns around into it: the top lines rest for the last two beats and the bass walks up. Uploaded parts play as uploaded in every section. Edits to the loop flow into every version. A new seed or length keeps your structure; a new style suggests one of its own. Click the ruler to jump. The roll keeps showing the loop, the notes of parts that are out don't glow, and the title line shows where you are ("Drop · bar 5 of 32").
- **Drag into your DAW** (Chrome and Edge on desktop): the **Drag into your DAW** tray at the bottom right of the stage has a chip for every part plus **All parts** (**Song** in song mode). Drag one onto a track in Ableton, FL Studio, Logic or any DAW, or onto the desktop, and it lands as a `.mid` of just that part, named like the export with the part on the end (`…_seed-9BADCC51-bass.mid`). **Export MIDI** drags out the whole loop, **Export song** the arrangement, and an upload's variation cards drag out one take each (**Export takes**, all of them). The file icons in the Tracks tab drag out their part too. The file is written when the drag starts, so it's what you hear, with swing and automation as in Export MIDI. Clicking still downloads. Other browsers can't hand a file to another app, so there the tray reads **Save as MIDI** and the chips just save.
- **Export song** writes the whole arrangement to one `-song.mid`: every part across every section, the automation and filter moves as CC, a marker at each section, and the guide beat as a General MIDI drum track on channel 10 (kick 36, clap 39 or snare 38 for rap, hats 42/46).
- **Import MIDI** (header button, or drop a `.mid` anywhere on the window) brings in your own part. The dialog lists the file's tracks and guesses what each one is: melody, counter-melody, brass, chords, bass, or leave it out (drums are left out). The guess comes from the track name, how many notes it plays at once and how low it sits; change any guess before importing. The key is detected from the parts and bars you keep, and changes as you change them. Bass and chord roots point to the tonic, the first bar's most of all, and the notes decide the scale. When another key fits nearly as well, it is offered beside the detected one; pick a key by hand and you can go back to the detected one. The dialog also shows the swing it heard and a loop length (2, 4 or 8 bars, plus which bars to take from a longer file). A pickup before bar 1 wraps round to the end of the loop. Notes are snapped to 16ths, and melody, counter, brass and bass keep one note at a time. Your parts stay in their lanes, the chords are read from them, and the other parts are written to fit. **Generate** and the dice rewrite only those other parts. Changing the key or scale moves your notes with it, edits included. The Tracks tab then shows **Your upload**: the original and six variation cards, each with a mini roll and a label for what changed. For melodies the moves include new notes on the same rhythm, a new rhythm for the same notes, syncopation, inversion, a new ending, passing notes, octave leaps and sequences. Bass lines get approach notes, root–fifth walks, pushed changes, new grooves, walk-ups and 808 slides. Chords get inversions, open voicings, added 7ths and 9ths, new stab rhythms, offbeat stabs, held chords and broken chords. Click a card to hear it in the loop (undo goes back). **How far** sets how many moves a card combines and how much each one changes. **New ideas** deals six more, and **Export takes** downloads the original and every card as tracks of one `.mid`. **Remove** lets go of the upload and generates every part again.

The guide beat (`B`) and the sounds are only there for playback. They aren't saved in the composition, and Export MIDI leaves the beat out. Your current idea, its arrangement and whether Song mode is on are autosaved to `localStorage`. Each note is its own small Web Audio graph (oscillators, filter, envelopes) that is freed as soon as it has faded out, so silent voices cost nothing. Chorus, drive, tremolo and the drum kit are native nodes too. Audio is scheduled 100 ms ahead with the browser's balanced latency setting, which keeps playback steady while the 3D view renders; the visuals are offset to match.

## Architecture

```
src/core/    Pure TypeScript, no DOM/audio/3D. The single source of truth.
  types.ts      NoteEvent { id, track, pitch, start, duration, velocity } in ticks (PPQ 480)
  plan.ts       The shared plan read by every part: drums, bar roles, energy, melody space, and how the bass and chords sit against the kick
  generator.ts  Seeded progression → chords → bass (best of four takes) → melody + phrasing → counter-melody → transition → feel → automation
  expression.ts The style's touch: energy dynamics, gated stabs, microtiming nudges
  score.ts      Structural metrics for melody, bass, chords and how the parts fit, and the scores that pick between takes
  develop.ts    Section materials: the loop moved on for A′, A″, B and reduced sections, and the turnaround into a drop
  locks.ts      Locked parts and bars, recompose around them, rewrite one bar, New chords
  takes.ts      Ranked takes on the melody or bass to audition
  transition.ts The end of an 8-bar loop: roll, stop or lift, for the beat and each part
  brass.ts      The brass part: a seeded plan (stabs, answers, swells or doubles, and where it comes in), written around the other parts
  harmony.ts    Creativity for chords: colours, borrowed chords, secondary dominants, passing chords, twists
  motif.ts      One-bar motifs (rhythm cells × contours) and their developments
  melody.ts     Phrase forms: plans bars as calls/answers/hooks, realises motifs over the chords (best of six takes)
  automation.ts Parameter ranges, the library of moves, and the suggestion picker
  styles.ts     Genre data: progressions, bass/chord patterns, accents, drum guide
  beat.ts       Picks the loop's beat from the style's figures by seed, and expands it into hits (shared by playback and song export)
  arrangement.ts Song structures per style, section timeline, song notes/beat/automation, section edits
  theory.ts     Scales, diatonic chords with idiomatic 7ths/9ths, names, key signatures
  groove.ts     Swing as a continuous tick mapping (shared by audio, render, export)
  midi.ts       Dependency-free Standard MIDI File writer
  readMidi.ts   Dependency-free Standard MIDI File reader (formats 0/1, running status; recovers files written without delta times)
  importing.ts  Uploads: role, key and swing detection, fitting into the loop, chords read from the parts, writing the rest around them
  variations.ts Seeded variation cards for an uploaded part, per role, and the named moves songs and bar rewrites use
  edit.ts       Immutable note edits with grid snapping and clamping
  validate.ts   Reads saved sessions and migrates older ones
src/eval/    The evaluation harness (`npm run eval`): metrics across styles and seeds, a report against the baseline, example MIDI files
src/state/   Zustand store, snapshot undo/redo with gesture + slider coalescing, persistence
src/audio/   Tone.js engine (lazy-loaded): note, beat and automation parts, per-part filter and sends, sidechain pump
  patches.ts    The instrument library as plain data (layers, inserts, levels) and each style's palette
  voice.ts      A one-shot native voice: sources, envelopes, release, and cleanup when it ends
  instruments.ts Plays a patch as per-note native voices (layers, glide, mono steal) through native inserts
  drums.ts      The guide-beat kit as native one-shot hits, and its per-style tuning
src/scene/   React Three Fiber roll: grid shader, notes, lane-aligned keyboard, playhead, automation deck, camera rig and framing
src/ui/      DOM panels, transport and shortcuts
src/theme.ts Track colours and scene palettes for the light and dark themes
```

The scene and the audio engine both read the same `Composition`, and neither one knows about the other. The scene gets a playback clock and an audition callback through context.

Generation is deterministic. The same settings and seeds always produce the same notes, and each part has its own seed derived from the master seed. The tests check scale membership, chord tones on strong beats (or suspensions that resolve by step), cadences, registers, monophony, density response, bass roots (or, in deep house, a chord's third) and part independence across every style × scale × length. Chord tones may come from a borrowed chord's own scale, and melody notes may approach the beat chromatically; the tests check both are labelled and resolve. Creativity tests check that 0 stays diatonic, that higher settings add colour, borrowed and passing chords, chromatic approaches and busier bass, and that every note outside the key is an approach or belongs to a borrowed chord. They also check that every phrase form labels its bars, that responses differ from their calls, and that traded answers never overlap the melody. Planning tests check that the chords play every bar alike outside the phrase ends, that the bass repeats its two-bar figure, that the counter-melody keeps to one figure, that the bass lands with some of the chord stabs but not all, and that a varied bar keeps its first half. Brass tests check that every loop has brass and every style uses every kind, that it stays in key and under the melody, that it comes in only where its plan says, that it leaves the answering to the counter-melody in a trade, that it follows a new melody, rerolls on its own, and that a loop saved before it existed gets one. Variety tests check that different seeds get different bass and chord rhythms (no one rhythm in more than a third of loops), that every style plays every kind of counter-melody, and that the beat varies by seed while keeping its backbeat, with no closed hat doubling an open hat or a roll. Riff tests check that riffs change pitch once and hold their repeated notes across the beats. Transition tests check that only 8-bar loops get one, that auto choices suit the style and repeat, that the beat rolls, stops or drops the kick in the last bar only, that fills play only with the full beat and land every 8 bars of the song, that each part plays its share (one hit then silence, stabs and a walk-up, held notes), that the first seven bars are untouched, and that a regenerated part keeps its share. For automation they check that suggestions are deterministic, stay in range, move, suit the style, keep on/off choices and land delay throws on phrase endings. MIDI tests check the byte layout and round-trip the notes and CC through `@tonejs/midi`, and that a dragged part carries only its own notes, intact, in the format Chromium turns into a file. Arrangement tests check every style's templates, that sections run end to end, that parts, drums and filter moves follow each section, that edits undo, and that the song export has its markers, a drum track and the full length. Import tests read back the app's own files and hand-built format-0 files. They check role guesses, key detection (across modes, from a bass line, under rootless chord voicings, and for the tonic of the app's own loops), files written without delta times, swing, pickups and tiling, and harmony read from chords, bass or a melody (including borrowed chords and half-bar changes). They also check that an upload survives Generate, key changes, length changes, undo and a reload. Variation tests check six distinct, deterministic cards for every part, all on the grid, in the loop and in range, single lines kept monophonic, and wider changes as the amount rises. Groove tests check the bass's kick windows, clap avoidance, ghost notes and two-bar figure for each style, and that the chords stay under the melody. Motif tests check that bars share their rhythm, that bars 2 and 3 build on bar 1, that sparse bars leave room, and that the melody holds when the chords or bass are rerolled. Expression tests check nudge limits, early stabs and late ghosts, energy dynamics, and that playback and export apply the nudge. Material tests check how sections choose their material, that A is the loop, that A′, A″ and B move it on, that reduced sections are sparser, the turnaround, and that uploads stay put. Lock tests check that locked parts and bars survive Generate, density, style, key and length changes, that edited parts follow the key and survive another part's reroll, that a bar rewrite touches only its bar, that New chords keeps a locked melody and picks a progression that suits it, that it all undoes, and that saves with and without locks load. Score tests check that the kept take is the best-scoring one of comparable density, that the plainest line doesn't win, that the accompaniment scores lower when it shadows or covers the melody, that every part offers distinct takes that load as shown, and that an 8-bar loop is written in well under 50 ms. Held-chord tests check that only deep and melodic house 8-bar loops hold their chords; ornament tests check that grace notes lead into long notes on the beat and that enclosures occur; phrasing tests check held notes, early releases, short grace notes and a tight rap line.
